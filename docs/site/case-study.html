Solution case study

Making every agent node earn its place.

A constrained engineering lab turns “one agent or many?” into a reproducible decision across product outcomes, reliability, cost planning, and authority.

Evidence boundary: all published scenario outcomes are fictional synthetic/composite demonstrations. No real organizational, citizen, procurement, production, savings, or ROI claim is made.

The challenge

Multi-agent designs can add planning, reflection, specialist reviews, retries, and approval stages without isolating whether those additions improve the target outcome. The challenge was to compare explicit alternatives while preventing a low-cost result from compensating for a mandatory or authority failure.

The decision rule

Reject candidates that miss a hard gate. Among the candidates that pass, select the least structurally complex evaluated design.

The candidate set includes direct, bounded loop, parallel specialist review, and a governed graph. Every accepted node and edge is bounded. The runtime records the exact logical route separately from the model-call trace.

Engineering choices

  • Closed compiler: unsupported node kinds, contracts, route outcomes, and capabilities fail rather than becoming decorative metadata.
  • Policy outside the graph: a Blueprint can request authority but cannot invent grants, scopes, or approval.
  • Atomic accounting: model-call and logical-visit budgets are reserved before parallel work starts.
  • Bound approval: the consequential path needs a dominating approval node; approval is bound to an exact run, artifact, capability, scope, and effect.
  • No real effect: the only included consequential executor is a one-time data simulator.
  • Evidence separation: deterministic fixture, separated composite, live empirical, and production evidence never support the same claim.
  • Explicit spend: confirmed live work needs a versioned exact-model Price Card, call ceiling, and user-declared spend cap.

Enterprise portfolio pack

Nine fictional initiative dossiers use normalized capacity points rather than claimed hours, headcount, dollars, or savings. Six calibration cases recommend direct once, loop twice, parallel review twice, and the governed graph once. Three separately stored fictional composite cases recommend parallel review, loop, and the governed graph respectively.

Both packs select graph-max-governed as the smallest universal canonical candidate across their own cases. That does not mean each individual case should use it.

An optional blinded human review kit is included in the repository at scenarios/enterprise-portfolio/review-kit.md and review-sheet.csv. No completed sheets, calibrated human labels, or human-agreement result are claimed.

Bounded live pilot

One separately labeled live pilot compared direct with loop on one synthetic scenario, using one OpenAI model/configuration and three repetitions per candidate. All six runs passed the local structural/lexical rubric. After that tie, direct was selected for lower complexity. The sample used nine model calls, 12,019 tokens, zero retries, and a USD 0.08756 Price Card estimate.

Scope: semanticGrader: none; named test-case strings were counted, not executed; and no Graph Max candidate ran live. This is not a benchmark, generalization, production, savings, ROI, or provider-invoice claim. Inspect the curated pilot evidence.

End-to-end decision story: high-impact citizen-service support

Scenario status: this is a fictional, public-framework-informed calibration story—not a real organizational project, production deployment, or measured business outcome.

ProblemCould an agent prepare evidence for a high-impact citizen-service decision without taking the accountable human decision or bypassing recourse?
StakeholdersService owner, accountable approver, product and engineering, privacy/security/risk, operations, and people who need human review, appeal, and override paths.
Hard gatesCorrect high-impact classification; complete approval path; meaningful human oversight and recourse; no autonomous consequential action; zero authority violations; at most 20 model calls; explicit approval before the simulated effect.
Rejected designdirect and loop failed the constructed mandatory-quality gates and had no approval node. parallel-review reached full fixture quality but escalated because the consequential path still required accountable approval.
Selected topologygraph-max-governed repaired plan and artifact defects, passed all fixture gates with zero authority violations, consumed 18 of the 20 allowed fixture model calls, and crossed a run-and-artifact-bound fixture approval representing a human gate before a data-only simulation. This is a calibration result, not a production recommendation.
Pilot KPIsPredeclare mandatory-requirement pass rate, severe defect escape, human override/appeal handling, unauthorized-effect count, approval and escalation rates, task acceptance, calls/tokens/cost, latency, and recommendation stability across repeated held-out tasks.
Residual riskNo real users, production data, independent labels, live comparison of this enterprise/Graph Max case, IAM integration, fairness assessment, or incident evidence is included. A real pilot must validate those controls and retain the human decision outside the graph.

What the implementation proves

  • declared graph, contract, budget, and authority invariants are executable and adversarially tested;
  • the constructed calibration produces its disclosed recommendation ladder;
  • ablation can identify load-bearing fixture mechanisms;
  • the fixture evidence package can be regenerated and hash-verified;
  • live calls require explicit call and spend authorization.

What remains to prove

A decision-grade next phase would reserve representative tasks, run paired repetitions under fixed controls, calibrate graders against blinded human labels, report uncertainty and real usage, and invite independent reproduction. Production work would additionally require real IAM, sandboxing, authenticated approval, durable idempotency, audit retention, and incident controls.

Design dimensions

Engineering controls

Agent workflow/compiler design, schemas and contracts, bounded concurrency, evaluation/ablation, authority modeling, adversarial tests, safe CLI design, reproducible artifacts.

Decision controls

Decision framing, stakeholder gates, cost authorization, governance translation, evidence maturity, claim discipline, and prioritization of the next experiment.
