The problem

Orchestration is easy to add. Evidence is harder.

More calls

Planning, review, repair, and synthesis consume model budget.

More latency

Sequential gates and retries extend the critical path.

More failure modes

Every edge, join, contract, and state transition can break.

More authority

Tools and effects widen the permission and approval surface.

The question is not “How many agents can we add?” It is “Which complexity is justified by a measured failure?”

Negative evidence

The interesting result is what breaks.

Ablation

Presentation, model synthesis, and speculative execution are removable in the built-in corpus. Remove plan review or security review, and named cases fail.

Authority

Unknown capabilities, approval bypass routes, and mutated grants fail closed. The only consequential executor is a one-time data simulator.

Every logical node visit and selected edge is recorded separately from the model-call trace.
Live plans require explicit confirmation plus call and spend ceilings before a credential is read.

What the evidence supports

Strong harness evidence. Decision-grade architecture evidence remains the next study.

A separate six-run live pilot exercised the bounded empirical path on one synthetic scenario. A credible next study reserves representative tasks, runs paired repetitions under fixed controls, calibrates graders to blinded human labels, and reports uncertainty, failures, real usage, and sensitivity.

Technical depth is not the number of agents. It is the quality of the decision, controls, and evidence.
